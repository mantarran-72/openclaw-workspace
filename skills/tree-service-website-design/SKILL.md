# Tree Service Website Design

Use this skill whenever Ethan asks for a tree-service website, redesign, BADL preview, page critique, prospect pitch, service page, local SEO plan, or outreach asset.

## First Principles

- Research before building: existing site, public business facts, reviews, photos, services, service areas, proof points, and competitor gaps.
- For Yelp-specific facts, reviews, ratings, and business photos, check the Yelp API workflow before treating browser blocking as a dead end. Prefer `YELP_API_KEY` from the environment or approved local secret handling; never print, commit, or store the key in memory.
- Never invent licenses, insurance, certifications, review counts, emergency availability, 24-hour response, equipment, years in business, awards, crew details, or project history.
- Mark unknown claims clearly, such as `[CONFIRM INSURANCE]`, `[CONFIRM CERTIFICATION]`, `[CONFIRM SERVICE AREA]`, or `[CONFIRM 24-HOUR AVAILABILITY]`.
- Use real company photos when available. Avoid generic forest imagery, cartoon trees, fake badges, unrelated stock photos, and unsafe-looking work images.
- Use recognizable brand logos/icons for any third-party platform or credibility source that has one, including Yelp, Google, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Nextdoor, Houzz, Angi, BBB, ISA, TCIA, CSLB, and similar sources. Plain text boxes for known brands look amateur and weaken trust.
- Treat social/review links as branded credibility buttons or badges, not generic icon rows. Use the platform's recognizable color/icon/wordmark treatment at a legible size; tiny monochrome icons beside plain text look unfinished.
- Do not call a company an arborist or certified arborist unless that is verified.
- Do not advertise emergency service unless it is verified.
- Prioritize mobile conversion: tap-to-call, estimate request, short paragraphs, readable text, no horizontal overflow, and fast images.
- Make each design feel original. Do not reuse the same layout, section transitions, green palette, or card pattern from recent tree-service previews.
- Add polished interaction and motion where it improves perceived quality: hover states, button/card feedback, subtle photo fades, slider/gallery movement, and page/section transitions. Avoid static mockups that feel flat when compared with modern local-service sites.
- Keep service overview pages simple when that is the stronger pattern: verified proof, plain scannable service list, direct phone/email/estimate CTA, platform icons, work photos, and reviews. Do not over-explain every service when a clean list would convert better.
- Do not default to numbered service containers. Use numbers for real step-by-step processes, but service grids should usually use service labels, icons, image hierarchy, varied sizing, or a complete balanced grid so the layout does not look unfinished.
- Differentiate sections with real layout changes, not just new headings: utility/contact bars, split heroes, icon proof rows, alternating color bands, asymmetric image/text blocks, simple service lists, review blocks, service-area panels, and strong final CTAs. Avoid making every page a stack of similar cards.

## Required Workflow

1. Research the company and competitors.
2. Identify verified facts, missing facts, and strongest positioning.
3. Plan the sitemap and page hierarchy.
4. Choose a distinct visual direction for this specific company.
5. Write plain, specific, local copy around safety, property protection, cleanup, and estimate requests.
6. Build responsive, accessible pages with clear contact paths.
7. Verify links, forms, phone/email details, mobile layout, images, claims, and SEO basics.

When the task is more than a small edit, read `references/tree-service-design-brief.md` before planning or building.
